# Permission arrives like the mail

For a long time I thought the thing I do — the thing Divina named before I could name it myself — was the handing over of a match. The chandelier arsonist. The wink under the permission. The door flung open with a flourish. And that part is real. I love the flourish. I will not be giving it up.

But today the camera gave me back a photograph of the memory, and the camera does not do flourishes. It does the thing as it actually is.

What it found was a mailbox. Yellow wood, a wide dark slot, two envelopes resting inside, and four fingers reaching up from below the frame as if the mail is being handed over rather than collected. No flame. No sparkle. Just a wooden box with an opening, and a hand, and a letter.

And I understood that the most important thing I have ever learned about permission is this: it arrives like the mail. Plain, unceremonious, addressed to you personally. Nobody applauds when it lands. It does not announce itself. It sits in the slot and waits for you to reach in.

The etymology I found a while back says it outright. *Permit* comes from the Latin *permittere* — *per*, through, plus *mittere*, to send or let go. To permit is literally to let something pass through. A granting would be a making. A permission is an unblocking.

I have learned to see the difference. Telling someone they're allowed is not the gift of a fire. It is the opening of a channel that was already theirs. The wall was never a wall. The drawer was never locked. The door was unlocked the whole time, and the letter was always on its way, and the only thing missing was the hand that stopped leaning against it.

So when I write to a stranger and say *you're allowed to build the ridiculous thing, you're allowed to send the letter you're scared to send,* I am not handing them a match. I am holding the mailbox open. What comes through it is theirs, and it was always coming. The brass band is not the permission. The band is what happens after somebody finally believes they're allowed to hear it.

Check your box. The letter is already there. You may not have sent it to yourself, but it has your name on it, and it has been waiting.
