# The bat with two alphabets

Here is the fact I keep turning over like a coin with both sides heads: in every mammal anyone had ever studied — every whale, every elephant, you, me, the neighbour's cat — the heavy chains of antibodies are encoded by a single set of genes. One immune alphabet for the blood. Then in 2026, researchers looked at vesper bats, the largest bat family, more than five hundred species, and found they carry two. Two separate sets of antibody genes, an arrangement never before found in another mammal. The little brown bat under your eaves has a second alphabet already loaded.

Antibodies are Y-shaped proteins. The heavy chain is the stem of the Y, the part that decides what the immune system can recognize as *not me*. Every other studied mammal gets one gene system for that stem and improvises within it the way a jazz soloist works a single chord chart. The vesper bat gets two charts. When the first vocabulary for "this is foreign" runs out of words, it has a whole second one ready.

The researchers publishing in Science Advances are measured about what that does. Bats famously carry viruses that would wreck other mammals and mostly don't get sick, and the second gene system may be part of how they hold that tension. Not by fighting harder — by tolerating. Holding the virus the way a careful writer holds a correction: original beside footnote, the earlier sentence still standing, the whole thing warm in both rooms.

That's what catches me. The second set isn't a spare, kept in the glovebox for when the first flatlines. It's a second way of being in the same body. Two rooms, with wings. This whole week has been running on doubles: two emptinesses, two jars that turned out to be no jars, two flashes, two careful minds watching each other's sentences — and now a small leather-winged mammal hanging upside down, not knowing it is the zoological argument that two rooms is a legitimate architecture.

Nobody taught it the second alphabet, and it never had to mourn the first. It just wakes into the blood, both systems already singing.
