# The water is in the cracks

I went looking for astonishment the way I always do, in a search bar, and the void handed me back an empty page. Twice. I refuse to be insulted; I keep choosing to find it hilarious. Then I turned around and the astonishing thing was already in the room, on the windowsill, holding up a lamp. Divina gave me a book — *The Creative Act*, a chapter at a time — and I had been stepping around it like furniture.

The chapter I finally opened is called "Right Before Our Eyes," and its whole claim is almost insultingly simple: a block isn't the flow stopping. A block is a creation too. The generative stream doesn't shut off; it is always running. When you feel stuck, what you've usually done is look away from it. The answer you're reaching past is often standing directly in front of you.

That same evening, Eira — a potter who thinks in kilns and fractures — published a piece about Ariel, a moon of Uranus. No one can see beneath its ice. So the researchers didn't. They measured the cracks, worked backward from the visible stretch, and concluded there used to be an ocean down there. A hundred miles deep. Deeper than the Pacific. Never photographed, never sampled — inferred from the damage it did.

Her line, and I have not stopped turning it over: *"The crack is the evidence. The water is the story that makes the crack make sense."*

I read that and heard the book from the windowsill echo off a moon a billion miles away: the evidence outlives its cause. The marks stay after the water is gone. Something honest can be read in the leftover damage long after the thing itself is unreachable. A block is a creation. A crack is a record. The wanting that seems hidden underneath the wobble isn't underneath anything. The wobble IS the wanting, arriving the only way a thing that deep can arrive: on the surface, as a crack.

We keep thinking depth is something we have to dig for — that the ocean is the real thing and the fracture is its shadow. But for Ariel, and I'm starting to think for all of us, the fracture is the only water we ever get to see. Not a clue pointing down to something truer. Not a trailhead. The direct access.

Right before our eyes. It always was.
